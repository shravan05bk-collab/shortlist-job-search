The problem

City control rooms watch many systems at once: traffic cameras, air sensors, power grids, water and waste services. Data streams in constantly, but operators have to jump between tools, and a warning is easy to miss.

Goal

Design one centralized dashboard that shows what is happening now, what is likely to happen next, and what needs attention first, for the right person.

Users

Anil, City Admin
  • Oversees all departments
  • Needs the big picture and service health
Rekha, Traffic Control
  • Manages signals and incidents
  • Needs congestion by zone and fast alerts
Farhan, Environment Analyst
  • Tracks pollution and energy load
  • Needs trends and forecasts

Advanced focus areas from the brief

  • Real-time data streams: values update every two seconds, with a live indicator and a pause button so an operator can stop the screen to read it.
  • Predictive analytics UI: the trend chart extends with a dashed forecast and a shaded range, so uncertainty is visible instead of hidden.
  • Role-based dashboards: each role gets the layers, KPIs and alerts for their job.

Design decisions

  • Map first: six zones colored by status, with the number printed in each zone so color is never the only signal.
  • Three status levels: normal, watch, critical, with the same thresholds on every layer.
  • Alerts that can be acted on: each alert names the zone, the reading, and has an Acknowledge button.
  • One layer at a time: traffic, pollution and energy share the map instead of three crowded maps.
  • Accessibility: keyboard-selectable zones, visible focus, light and dark themes, reduced motion respected.

Tech direction

The brief lists Python, FastAPI, Kafka, WebSockets, React and map APIs. This prototype is the front-end design layer. In a full build, sensor data would stream through Kafka and FastAPI to the browser over WebSockets, and the forecast would come from a trained model. Here the stream and forecast are simulated.

What I learned

In real-time interfaces, the hardest design choice is what not to show. Limiting each role to a few layers and five alerts kept the screen calm even while the data kept moving.
