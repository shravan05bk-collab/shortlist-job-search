Pulseboard

One analytics dashboard for many companies. Each role sees the tools they need and nothing more.

    The problem

    Businesses that use a SaaS analytics product have teams with very different needs. A finance admin, a team manager, and a data analyst all open the same dashboard, and most of it is noise for each of them. Charts are dense, and people can’t tell what changed or what to do next.

    Industry focus: B2B SaaS

    Key challenges from the brief: complex data visualization, role-based dashboards (Admin, Manager, Analyst), and customizable widgets. The design handles each one directly.

    Users

    Meera, Admin
    • Manages the company account
    • Needs the whole picture and user access control
    Karthik, Manager
    • Tracks team revenue and orders
    • Needs a quick read of what moved this month
    Divya, Analyst
    • Digs into regions and accounts
    • Needs clean charts and CSV export

    Design decisions

    • Multi-tenant switcher: one control at the top switches the whole dashboard between companies, so there is no confusion about whose data this is.
    • Role-based views: the role decides which widgets appear. Admins also get user management. Analysts get export. Each role sees a short note explaining what they can do.
    • Summary first: four KPI cards with change versus the previous period come before any chart.
    • Simple charts: one line chart for the trend, one bar chart for volume, one ranked bar list for regions. Every chart has labeled axes, no 3D, and no decoration.
    • Customizable widgets: show, hide, and reorder, saved per role.
    • Responsive and accessible: two columns on desktop, one on mobile, tables scroll sideways, focus outlines, light and dark theme.

    Process

    Research on tools like Mixpanel, Metabase and Power BI, personas, role and permission map, wireframes, high-fidelity screens, then this clickable prototype. Data in the prototype is sample data.

    What I learned

    Role-based design is mostly about removing things. Once each role saw only what it needed, the dashboard felt simpler without losing any power.
